Part 1 Instructions

You will now begin the first of two parts of this survey.

In Part 1, you will be presented brief statements accompanied by 24 charts. If a chart is too small or hard to read, you can zoom in by clicking the image. There is an example image below to practice this interaction, if you would like.

Your completion time will be measured, but there is no time limit on your responses. For each statement, you will be asked if the statement is accurate or not.

Some statements will explicitly state information found within the chart, such as the chart type or values within the chart, while other statements may use outside information to contextualize or understand the chart.

For the purposes of your response, assume that the chart and its data are accurate. If the statement uses outside information, use your outside knowledge of the topic for your answer.

If a statement contains one or more fallacies, then mark the statement as inaccurate. Specifically look out for:

  • Correlation-causation fallacies, where one factor is assumed to cause another unrelated factor.
  • Cherry-picking, by applying one specific value to a broader category (such as saying that a German data point can be applied to all of Europe).
  • Setting an arbitrary threshold, by saying that some arbitrary number is "too much" or "too little" without basis.

If you believe an answer may be correct, you may use your judgement to guess. If you are completely unsure, you may select "I don't know" and continue with the rest of the items.
